## Projects

### 1. Land-cover change along the SH 29 corridor, Central Texas
**Type:** Undergraduate research (OSU URSA)  
**Role:** First author  
**Dates:** January 2026 – present  
**Status:** Manuscript submitted to JEOGA, August 2026 · research showcase / symposium presentation  
**Co-author:** Peder Nelson

Rapid development along SH 29 has converted farmland and woodland into subdivisions and commercial centers. This project documents that change from 2016 to 2026 with two complementary methods.

**Methods**
- 37 Collect Earth Online primary sample units (100 m × 100 m), 100 points interpreted in each unit from high-resolution imagery
- Annual USDA LCMS land-cover and change metrics for developed land, agriculture/grassland, tree canopy, and mechanical clearing
- Comparison of CEO labels with ESA WorldCover and USGS LCMAP at the same plots

**Results**
- Developed land increased from about 5–8% (2016) to about 50–52% (2026)
- Tree canopy declined from about 48% to 22%
- Peak clearing around 2022, aligned with Santa Rita Ranch and related construction
- Of 37 sample units, 46% remained tree-dominated, and 19% were already primarily built-up

**Files in this repo**
- Manuscript PDF
- Key figures (plot map, 2016 vs 2026 comparison)
- Summary table of LCMS trends

**Citation**  
Bright, J. D., & Nelson, P. *Documenting Rapid Land-Cover Change Along the SH 29 Corridor, Central Texas, Using Collect Earth Online and LCMS Data.* Manuscript submitted to JEOGA, 2026.

### 2. West Virginia coal mining StoryMap
**Type:** Course term project  
**Role:** Sole author  
**Year:** 2026  
**Live map:** https://arcg.is/1CTyrn3

StoryMap synthesizing published research and public spatial data on the environmental and economic legacy of coal mining in West Virginia. Covers globalization, uneven development, and local environmental effects, with locator and thematic maps.

This is a literature-and-cartography project, not original image classification. It shows how I structure a public GIS narrative in ArcGIS StoryMaps.
